Step 2: Choose Your Training Software (No-Code Frameworks)

Because writing raw PyTorch training loops from scratch requires advanced machine learning degrees, the open-source community has built excellent graphic and command-line training tools that handle the math for you.

Option A: Unsloth (Highly Recommended for Your RTX 5070)

Unsloth is a local training framework specifically optimized to minimize VRAM usage. It makes training 2x faster and reduces memory overhead by 80%. With Unsloth, you can easily train a 3B parameter model LoRA directly on your 8GB RTX 5070 card without running out of memory.

Option B: LLaMA-Factory (Web Browser UI)

If you prefer a full graphical interface with buttons, sliders, and drop-down menus instead of typing Python scripts, use LLaMA-Factory. It spins up a local web page where you can upload your dataset.jsonl, select your model, adjust your target dimensions, and click a big "Start Training" button.

Step 3: Configure the LoRA Matrix Parameters

When you open your training software, you will be prompted to enter specific values for the LoRA matrix. Use these optimized settings for a text-based problem-solving chatbot:
• LoRA Rank (r): 16 or 32. (Higher numbers let the model learn more complex internal patterns, but take up more VRAM).
• LoRA Alpha: 32 or 64. (Typically set to exactly double your Rank value; this controls the structural scaling weight of your training data).
• Target Modules: You must specify which internal parts of the neural network to train. For general text chatbots, target the attention layers: q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, o_proj, gate_proj, up_proj, down_proj.

Step 4: Run the Training Process

When you start the script, the engine will loop through your dataset multiple times (called Epochs). You will watch a terminal graph or metric called the Loss Value drop over time.
• At the beginning, the Loss might be high (e.g., 2.5).
• As the chatbot learns to match your exact expected problem-solving style, the Loss will drop.
• Target: Stop training when your loss stabilizes between 0.5 and 1.0. (If it drops below 0.2, the model is "overfitting"—it will just memorize your data word-for-word instead of thinking critically).
